展开摘要

arXiv:2611.15105v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Vector databases are increasingly deployed as retrieval pipelines in which representation, indexing, and query-time execution jointly determine accuracy, throughput, and cost. To address the above limitation, we introduce VecDBLens, an Evaluatology-based framework that treats the function under diagnosis as an evaluated object and all remaining dataset, workload, parameter, and runtime factors as matched evaluation conditions. VecDBLens attributes performance differences to individual evaluated objects through comparisons under matched evaluation conditions, summarizing their performance and stability across multiple datasets and workloads. First, retrieval performance is fundamentally upper-bounded by embedding quality, revealing that many apparent indexing failures are therefore representation-side limitations. Second, stronger index parameterization is not always beneficial: overly aggressive configurations can reshape neighborhood topology and cause recall regression. These results challenge the common practice of evaluating vector databases primarily through indexing and show that real-world limitations arise from constraints across retrieval functions rather than any single function alone. VecDBLens provides a principled basis for identifying the true performance drivers of vector databases and for guiding their design in practical applications.

arXiv:2611.14697v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Tables are compositional, with content spread across a schema, cell values, rows, and columns. Large-scale operations such as retrieval, overlap estimation, deduplication, and data curation need these local signals composed into one reusable embedding vector per table. Table representation methods, however, are mostly evaluated through row-level prediction or supervised downstream tasks, which do not measure table-level embedding quality directly. We introduce a benchmark that evaluates table-level embeddings through four desiderata: consistency under partial views, discriminability across label granularities, robustness to benign perturbations, and efficiency. Across twelve methods on synthetic and real corpora, lightweight schema- and text-based fingerprints are among the strongest. The sharpest contrast comes from two tabular foundation models, TabPFN and ConTextTab: although both are strong row-level predictors, their states, composed by mean, max, or three further parameter-free pooling rules, trail the strongest fingerprints. We therefore ask whether the gap lies in the encoder or in the compositional rule. Keeping ConTextTab frozen, we learn only the compositional rule over its cell states, using support-conditioned distribution matching (SCDM), a self-supervised loss we propose that matches the distribution of embedding similarities to the distribution of partition overlaps within and across tables.
