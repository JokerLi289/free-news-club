ADIndex: Layer-Adaptive Sparse Attention Indexing via Group-Level Distillation
展开摘要

arXiv:2611.12956v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) reduces the computational cost of attention through token-level selection. However, its indexer still scores the entire causal prefix for each query, making indexing a dominant source of latency in long-context inference. Notably, the indexer alone can account for more than 50% of total prefill GPU time at long context lengths. To address this bottleneck, we propose ADIndex, an Adaptive and Distillation-driven Indexing method for token-level sparse attention. ADIndex integrates three key components: (i) a layer-wise calibration strategy that adaptively accommodates cross-query retrieval overlap, motivated by our observation of pronounced inter-layer heterogeneity; (ii) a group-level distillation scheme that leverages this heterogeneity to efficiently train adaptive layer-wise group indexers, capturing salient retrieval signals from individual queries; and (iii) a low-latency inference strategy that reduces repeated full-prefix scans through token bank sharing within query groups while retaining query-specific refinement. Experiments on DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5.1 demonstrate that ADIndex preserves near-lossless task performance on LongBench, LongBench v2, and RULER while delivering substantial efficiency gains over standard DSA, including prefill and decoding kernel speedups of up to 6.22x and 3.40x, respectively, at a context length of 256K across the layer-wise reuse configurations, as well as an end-to-end serving speedup of 1.54x at a context length of 128K.

展开摘要

arXiv:2611.13069v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Banks now train foundation models on ordered sequences of customer events such as transactions, app sessions, and authorizations. Deployed, such a model predicts forward in time, often for customers and merchants absent from training, yet each is evaluated on private data, so no two can be compared. Public benchmarks for such sequences rarely test a period the model has not seen; recommendation studies usually hold out each user's last events instead. We present INTENT (INTEraction, Novelty, and Transfer evaluation for next-action prediction), a public benchmark that scores a model in seven cells defined by whether the user, the period, and the target item appeared in training. An audit of 59 public dataset families retains five datasets; the most common defect is the lack of a calendar timestamp that orders events. No date lies within the time span of all five datasets, so each is split at its own cutoff, chosen by one rule fixed before any model is evaluated. Summed over the datasets, every cell holds at least 111,769 users. In reference results the cell determines the best model: a transformer leads on seen items after the cutoff but scores zero on new items, a variant that embeds item metadata helps mainly on MicroLens, and repeating the user's last item leads on new items in four of five datasets. This parameter-free control also shows that on three datasets most of the score lost after the cutoff reflects changed behavior rather than the model. 展开摘要

We compare task ranks on aligned support, train five backbones under all seven non-empty objective combinations on five datasets with three seeds, and contrast fixed-model profiles with specialist portfolios. Fairness-Aware Retrieval Optimization for Retrieval-Augmented Generation
展开摘要

arXiv:2605.15790v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds large language models (LLMs) in external knowledge, but the retrieved documents can systematically skew generated answers toward one group. This effect is difficult to analyze and control in top-k settings, where several documents at different context positions jointly shape the output. We present a three-stage framework for fairness-aware retrieval in top-k RAG. First, a probabilistic reranker sets the group composition of the retrieved context at each position, enabling controlled perturbation of retrieval bias without modifying the retriever. Second, we show that output bias is well approximated by a position-weighted linear function of position-wise retrieval bias, revealing model-specific positional sensitivities. Third, we use this model to formulate retrieval as an optimization problem that trades off relevance against predicted output bias, and propose FARO, a scalable solution to that problem which decomposes the globally coupled quadratic objective into independent per-query assignment problems through a Fenchel--Legendre dual reformulation. Experiments with four open LLMs, and political and gender bias as use cases show that the linear model predicts output bias on unseen configurations in 17 of 24 settings. FARO substantially reduces output bias in most settings, while, compared with a linear-programming baseline, FARO achieves similar relevance, remains feasible where the baseline does not, and scales better to larger query sets and candidate pools.

展开摘要

arXiv:2611.13209v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Language models increasingly power data-intensive applications, from semantic data processing to question answering and agentic workflows. Using frontier models for every request is prohibitively expensive, while relying only on smaller models reduces accuracy. We study model routing under a user-specified cost budget, aiming to maximize accuracy under task-specific correctness criteria. Existing routers fall short in two ways: pretrained routers rely on generic preference signals that may not reflect a task's own correctness criteria or a new model pair, and model cascades run the weaker model first on every request, adding sequential latency. We present ARoute, a task-aligned router that learns cost and accuracy signals to select a model for each request before inference. ARoute has two variants: ARoute-Batch operates on a batch of requests known in advance, routing requests with the greatest expected benefit to the stronger model subject to the budget, whereas ARoute-Stream processes requests online, using a threshold calibrated on a small validation set to satisfy the cost quota and dynamically adjusting it as the budget is consumed. For ARoute-Stream, we provide a finite-sample guarantee on expected normalized spending. We evaluate ARoute on MMLU-Pro, LiveCodeBench, SuperGPQA, and two value-filling tasks built from SWAN semantic query benchmark against other state-of-the-art approaches. At matched cost, ARoute-Batch achieves the highest mean accuracy in all 15 task-cost settings, improving over the strongest baseline by 4.1 percentage points on average and by up to 11.9 points. ARoute also completes a batch up to 2.8x faster than cascades.
