SJG: A Skyline Joint Graph for Multi-Component Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search
展开摘要

arXiv:2611.29399v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Graph-based indexes enable efficient approximate nearest neighbor search at scale by linking similar objects. In applications such as product retrieval and rental listing search, similarity depends on several components whose relative importance varies across queries. The challenge is that the index must select neighbors during construction, but the query weights that determine which objects are closest are not yet known. A straightforward approach is to index each component separately. However, merging the results of these searches can miss the nearest neighbor under the weighted distance. A joint graph allows a search to follow links across components, helping to avoid such misses, but selecting neighbors separately within each component can leave redundant edges, and keeping component hierarchies synchronized limits parallel construction. To address these limitations, we propose the Skyline Joint Graph (SJG). Although query weights can change the ranking of candidate neighbors, the dominance relation among them is independent of those weights. SJG exploits this property by pooling candidates across components and retaining the Skyline for each node, thereby enabling efficient approximate nearest neighbor search under different query weights while supporting parallel graph construction. Extensive experiments on six real-world datasets demonstrate that SJG achieves up to $27.9\times$ the query throughput of baseline methods at 90% Recall@10, while accelerating single-threaded index construction by up to $201\times$. Its index requires at least 36% less space than the smallest baseline index.

展开摘要

PAMS tracks a low-rank approximation of the dominant activation subspace and its associated spectrum online, and uses the spectrum to softly reweight perturbation variance while preserving exploration in directions outside the retained subspace. A trace normalization separates the perturbation geometry from its overall scale, enabling consistent perturbation magnitudes across layers. We show theoretically that the resulting perturbation covariance induces right-side preconditioning of the gradient of the corresponding Gaussian-smoothed objective. We further characterize the estimator's smoothing bias and second moment and derive a nonconvex convergence guarantee that captures the effects of the perturbation geometry and smoothing radius. Experiments on transformer-based language-model fine-tuning tasks across GLUE and SuperGLUE demonstrate that PAMS improves the effectiveness and stability of zeroth-order optimization while retaining its gradient-free and objective-evaluation-based advantages.

arXiv:2611.28465v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Irregular time series are recorded at nonuniform times and are often queried at other times: between observations, after the last one, or on a regular grid. Continuous-time models answer such queries by integrating latent dynamics, and time-aware attention models answer them by revisiting the observation tokens. We introduce HiPPO Functional Memory (HiPPO-FM), an attention operator that writes an irregular history into a rank-M function space with explicit time coordinates. Masked self-attention first exchanges content among the observations; the updated values are then contracted against kernel-smoothed HiPPO-Legendre features and solved through a regularized Gram matrix, so the retained coefficients define a function that is read at any time through the same features.
