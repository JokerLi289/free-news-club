展开摘要

Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal for Schema Retrieval in Text-to-SQL
展开摘要

arXiv:2611.20979v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLM-based natural-language interfaces to relational databases are gated by a schema-retrieval step: the generator can only be as good as the slice of schema it is shown. On a database with hundreds of tables, that slice must be selected and the selection is two-sided---omit a needed table and the query becomes unrecoverable, include too many and the irrelevant ones act as noise that degrades the model's output. When an agentic retriever that lets the model inspect the schema can be too costly or too slow, the fallback is an embedder that ranks tables by cosine similarity and keeps a top-k. This fallback has two weaknesses: a fixed budget is suboptimal question by question, and embedding similarity overlooks foreign-key-linked tables that are necessary for joins. We present Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal (AEGT, ElbowHop for short), a property-graph table retriever that addresses both: an adaptive elbow cutoff replaces the fixed budget with a per-question one under a single aggressiveness parameter, and bidirectional foreign-key traversal deterministically recovers the bridge tables embedding similarity misses. We evaluate the retriever on 3 corpora of increasing candidate-pool size (BIRD Mini-Dev queried per database, 3--13 tables; its eleven schemas merged, 74; and a merged Spider-train graph, 546) and isolate the two mechanisms under a per-question budget-matched protocol. Across these settings the adaptive elbow emerges as the dominant mechanism---at matched recall it roughly doubles precision over a fixed budget on the larger pools, the margin widening as the pool grows---while the foreign-key traversal pays off on the join-critical bridge tables dense retrieval cannot reach, in the single-hop tight-budget case. We characterize the method as a low-cost, recall-robust first stage for schema retrieval on which a downstream selection or generation step can build.

展开摘要

Across multiple benchmarks, HiMX reduces the average end-to-end accuracy loss from 2.38 to 1.77 points on Qwen3-8B and from 2.23 to 1.85 points on Qwen3-30B-A3B, demonstrating the potential benefit of heterogeneous mantissa representations for W4A4 LLM inference.

Check Token: Real-Time Self-Verification and Precise Truncation in LLM Reasoning
展开摘要

arXiv:2611.18564v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: ECC memory embeds 8 parity bits for every 64 data bits and automatically detects and corrects errors on each read. The parity bits carry no data and only safeguard integrity, at ~12.5% overhead. Yet the reasoning chains of large language models lack such built-in self-verification: once an error occurs it propagates along the chain, and existing methods can only verify externally after generation completes. We propose the check token, establishing built-in self-verification for language model generation streams for the first time: a functional marker is added to the vocabulary, and the model triggers self-checking (analysis, localization, truncation, rewriting) by outputting it at any position. The check token carries no reasoning content (discarded after triggering) and only safeguards reasoning correctness, directly corresponding to the role of ECC parity bits, also at only ~13% overhead. Speculative forking further eliminates false-positive latency, and segmented reward makes trigger timing end-to-end learnable. Experiments (Qwen3-32B / Qwen3-Next) show that the check token achieves +10.8 pp improvement on HMMT25 at 1.13x overhead, with token efficiency 88x that of Best-of-8, and the precise truncation advantage monotonically increases with chain length.
